# ParticleNet Interpolation — Uncertainties

Which nuisances the ParticleNet layer adds on top of the Baseline
interpolation, the gates that decided them, the rule that sizes them, and
their values. Method context: [METHOD.md](METHOD.md); the Baseline families
this sits on: [../UNCERTAINTY.md](../UNCERTAINTY.md).

Everything is **measured, not assumed**, at the frozen working point
eps_B = 20%, over 5 mHc x 2 channels x 2 run periods (84 shape pairs, 336
yield points, all production-relevant |dmA| <= 2.5). Source of record:
`test/pnet_interp/pnet_uncertainties.json`, regenerated by
`python3 test/pnet_interp/exportPnetUncertainties.py`.

## What this layer may cover — and what it must not

The ParticleNet templates inherit the Baseline shape surfaces **and** the
Baseline yield model, so the Baseline `CMS_interp_*` nuisances already apply
in full. The only thing this layer may add is the error the score cut
introduces on top of them.

That distinction is load-bearing for the yield. The measured residual splits
into two independent terms:

| term | what it is | who covers it |
|---|---|---|
| `r_model` | Baseline yield model -> ParticleNet sample dirs | Baseline `CMS_interp_norm` — **already** |
| `r_eps` | the eps(mA) interpolation | this layer's `CMS_interp_eff_pnet` |

Sizing the ParticleNet eff nuisance on `r_total` would double-count the
Baseline error. It is sized on `r_eps` alone.

## The families

| family | measures | applied as | nuisance name |
|---|---|---|---|
| res | \|σ_eff_cut/σ_eff_nocut − 1\| | σL,σR scaled by (1 ± value) | `CMS_interp_res_pnet_{ch}_{13TeV\|13p6TeV}` |
| eff | \|eps_interp/eps_direct − 1\| | lnN (1 + value) on each era's signal column | `CMS_interp_eff_pnet_{ch}_{13TeV\|13p6TeV}` |

- **There is no scale family.** Gate U1 below.
- Names are **period-level**, matching the Baseline convention: one nuisance
  spans a run period's four era columns, decorrelated between periods.
- res values are per (channel, run period). eff values are per (channel,
  era), carried as columns of one period-level lnN row — again the Baseline
  structure.
- **No mA binning.** The Baseline norm is binned at 15/80/100/155; the
  ParticleNet reach [82.5, 97.5] lies entirely inside the single `onZ` bin,
  so the binning has nothing to resolve here.
- Values are keyed by the production channel (`SR1E2Mu`, `SR3Mu`), not by
  study pairing: every ParticleNet point has mHc >= 100 and mA >= 70, so
  SR3Mu always resolves to the highM pairing and the ambiguity the Baseline
  arm has to handle cannot arise.

## Gate U1 — bias or refit noise?

**The gate.** The score cut removes 40–60% of the events, so the post-cut DCB
is refit on a subset and its parameters move by pure statistics. Before
sizing anything, ask whether each family's shift is a *shift* at all: take
the SIGNED mean (noise averages to zero, a bias does not) and compare
`d_scale` against its own refit statistical error. Because the cut sample is
a **subset** of the no-cut sample, the variance of the difference is
approximately `|err_cut² − err_nocut²|`, not their sum.

**The measurement** (eps_B = 20%, 84 pairs):

| family | channel | period | signed mean | error | n_sigma | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| scale | SR1E2Mu | Run2 | −0.0005 | 0.0028 | 0.2 | noise |
| scale | SR1E2Mu | Run3 | +0.0047 | 0.0049 | 1.0 | noise |
| scale | SR3Mu | Run2 | +0.0155 | 0.0057 | 2.7 | noise |
| scale | SR3Mu | Run3 | −0.0013 | 0.0060 | 0.2 | noise |
| **scale** | **ALL** | | **+0.0046** | 0.0026 | **1.8** | **noise** |
| res | SR1E2Mu | Run2 | −0.0120 | 0.0016 | 7.3 | BIAS |
| res | SR1E2Mu | Run3 | −0.0087 | 0.0019 | 4.6 | BIAS |
| res | SR3Mu | Run2 | −0.0087 | 0.0016 | 5.6 | BIAS |
| res | SR3Mu | Run3 | −0.0090 | 0.0023 | 3.9 | BIAS |
| **res** | **ALL** | | **−0.0096** | 0.0009 | **10.4** | **BIAS** |

`|d_scale|` against its own refit statistical error: median pull **1.16**,
44% within 1σ, 76% within 2σ.

**The decisions.**

- **scale gets no nuisance.** Its spread is the refit statistics — a median
  pull of ~1 is what pure noise looks like, and no channel x period cell
  reaches 3σ. Sizing a nuisance on it would double-count MC statistical
  error that autoMCStats already carries in every bin. Its apparent excess
  over the Baseline `CMS_interp_scale` in some cells is noise inflation, not
  a physical shift.
- **res gets a nuisance covering bias AND scatter.** The −0.96% narrowing is
  10σ overall and coherent across all four cells, with no cell escaping.
  Physically sensible: core, well-measured events pass a score cut slightly
  more often than tail events, so the surviving peak is marginally narrower.
  It is a genuine bias, and per the frozen decision it is **not corrected** —
  the Baseline σ surfaces are reused untouched — so the nuisance must absorb
  the bias as well as the spread around it. That is why the rule below takes
  the rms about **zero**, never about the mean.

## Gate U2 — the rule

Two candidates were computed on the same residuals: the Baseline
rms-then-max, and a plain p90/max envelope. They agree to within ~15% in
every cell, so consistency with the Baseline arm decides it.

> the **rms within each mHc study**, then the **max across studies** holding
> at least 2 points, floored by the cell's **pooled rms**, then by an
> absolute floor (res 0.01, eff 0.01).

Residuals are taken about **zero**, so a biased family is covered
bias-and-scatter together by the same expression (Gate U1).

Comparison at the decision point:

| family | cell | rms-max | p90 | max | adopted | Baseline in the same cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| res | SR1E2Mu/Run2 | 0.0192 | 0.0218 | 0.0286 | 0.0192 | 0.020 |
| res | SR1E2Mu/Run3 | 0.0155 | 0.0214 | 0.0220 | 0.0155 | 0.018 |
| res | SR3Mu/Run2 | 0.0208 | 0.0189 | 0.0272 | 0.0208 | 0.014 |
| res | SR3Mu/Run3 | 0.0211 | 0.0211 | 0.0245 | 0.0211 | 0.015 |

### Why res is not simply shared with the Baseline

Reusing `CMS_interp_res` and dropping the ParticleNet term was considered.
It fails, and it fails asymmetrically:

| channel | period | Baseline res | ParticleNet res | ratio | sharing would |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SR1E2Mu | Run2 | 0.0200 | 0.0192 | 0.96 | be conservative |
| SR1E2Mu | Run3 | 0.0180 | 0.0155 | 0.86 | be conservative |
| SR3Mu | Run2 | 0.0140 | **0.0208** | **1.49** | **under-cover by 49%** |
| SR3Mu | Run3 | 0.0150 | **0.0211** | **1.41** | **under-cover by 41%** |

"the ParticleNet res is smaller, so the Baseline value is conservative"
holds for SR1E2Mu only. SR3Mu is where the Baseline res is *smallest* in the
whole analysis (1.4–1.5%), so the ParticleNet term overtakes it there.

There is a second reason the two cannot substitute for each other: they are
different quantities. `CMS_interp_res` is the error of the sigma surface at
an interpolated mA. The ParticleNet res is the bias from reusing an UNCUT
surface for a CUT template — it is present on the diagonal (seed predicting
itself), where no interpolation happens at all. Both apply to a ParticleNet
template, and being independent they add in quadrature.

## Gate U3 — review before adopting

Mirror of the Baseline Gate C. Read `pnet_uncertainties.json` in this order:

1. `driver` per cell, with `per_study_rms` for how far the others sit below
   it. A cell driven by one study is that study's limitation.
2. `studies_below_min_points` — studies skipped for holding < 2 points.
3. `n_points` — with only 3 anchors per mHc, a thin cell is easy to make.
4. Any value sitting exactly on its floor: the floor is then setting the
   number rather than catching a degenerate cell. Currently **none**.
5. `on_absolute_floor` across all cells — currently all false, so the floors
   are pure safety nets.

Run3 eff cells above 5% are expected and are **not** this layer's error:
they track the same upstream per-sample yield scatter that inflates the
Baseline Run3 norm ([../EXPERIMENTS.md](../EXPERIMENTS.md), "Run3
normalization scatter").

## Evidence

**The working point changed the answer.** Freezing eps_B = 20% instead of
argmax-Z improved every efficiency-interpolation figure, most where it was
worst:

| | argmax Z | eps_B = 20% |
|---|---|---|
| `r_eps` anchors LOO median / p90 / max | 0.0140 / 0.0512 / 0.1430 | **0.0127 / 0.0378 / 0.0841** |
| `r_eps` blind validation median / p90 / max | 0.0063 / 0.0272 / 0.0522 | **0.0053 / 0.0173 / 0.0259** |
| `r_eps` MHc100 median / max | 0.0298 / 0.1430 | **0.0251 / 0.0740** |
| `\|d_res\|` median / p90 / max | 0.0120 / 0.0252 / 0.0525 | **0.0091 / 0.0215 / 0.0286** |

The categories that carried the low-B threshold spikes were the same ones
carrying the worst `r_eps`, which is why one change fixed both.

**The new term is the small one.** At eps_B = 20%, on the blind validation
points (MHc115_MA87, MHc145_MA92 — the only trained mA off the 85/90/95
lattice):

| residual | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r_eps` (this layer) | **0.0053** | 0.0173 | 0.0259 |
| `r_model` (inherited Baseline) | 0.0241 | 0.0796 | 0.1043 |
| `r_total` | 0.0241 | 0.0863 | 0.1103 |

Split by period, `r_model` is 0.7% median in Run2 and 6.6% in Run3 — the
known upstream Run3 scatter, not something ParticleNet introduces. **The
ParticleNet layer contributes roughly a fifth of the total yield error**;
the interpolation is dominated by the Baseline arm it sits on.

**Anchor closure is leave-one-out.** Each anchor is predicted from the other
two, so the 85 and 95 endpoints are extrapolations rather than
interpolations — which is why anchor residuals exceed the blind validation
ones. The honest out-of-sample number is the validation row.

## Known limitations

- **Two blind validation points.** MHc115_MA87 and MHc145_MA92 are the only
  genuinely out-of-sample tests; everything else is leave-one-out on three
  anchors.
- **Three anchors per mHc** is the binding constraint on eps(mA). A quadratic
  through three points has no degrees of freedom left, so the model cannot be
  tested for goodness of fit at the anchors — only through LOO.
- **MHc100 sets most eff cells**, so the values inherit its statistics.
- **Uncalibrated pulls.** Like the Baseline arm, the closure pulls are not
  calibrated; the exported envelopes use relative residuals and are
  unaffected.
