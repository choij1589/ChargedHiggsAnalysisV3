# Interpolation — Uncertainties

Which nuisances the interpolation chain derives, the rule that sizes
them, their current production values, and the measured evidence behind
the rule. Workflow context: [WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md); the model
experiments: [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md).

Everything here is **derived from the leave-one-out closures, not
assumed**: each of the 78 LOO fits removes one mass point from BOTH
surfaces (shape and yield) and closes shape + yield at that point; the
residuals are the interpolation error sampled at genuine
one-point-removed predictions. Source of record:
`configs/interpolation_uncertainties.json` (git-tracked; regenerated by
`exportInterpUncertainties.py --loo --all --pooled --write-config`).

## The three families

| family | measures | applied as | nuisance name |
|---|---|---|---|
| scale | \|x0_pred − x0_direct\| / σ_eff_direct | x0 shift by ±value·σ_eff | `CMS_interp_scale_{ch}_{13TeV\|13p6TeV}` |
| res | \|σ_eff_pred/σ_eff_direct − 1\| | σL,σR scaled by (1 ± value) | `CMS_interp_res_{ch}_{13TeV\|13p6TeV}` |
| norm | \|N_pred/N_meas − 1\| per era | lnN (1 + value) on each era's signal column | `CMS_interp_norm_{ch}_{13TeV\|13p6TeV}` |

- **All three names are period-level** — one nuisance spans the four era
  columns of its run period, decorrelated between periods (adopted
  2026-08-12; the measured cross-era correlation below is the evidence).
  A single lnN row carries per-era values in its columns, so norm keeps
  the measured per-era sizes under one coherent parameter.
- scale/res values: per (channel, run period), NOT binned in mA, no mHc
  dependence.
- norm values: per (channel, era), binned in mA at **15 / 80 / 100 / 155**
  (belowZ / onZ / aboveZ; `NORM_MA_BINS`), no mHc dependence. mA outside
  [15, 155] raises. The target mA selects the bin; the bin never appears
  in the name — one datacard holds one mass point, so only one bin per
  channel can occur in a workspace.
- **Keying asymmetry**: values are keyed by STUDY channel
  (SR3Mu_lowM/SR3Mu_highM) because a single mA bin can contain both
  pairings, while the nuisance NAME uses the production channel `SR3Mu` —
  safe because one datacard holds one mass point, which resolves to
  exactly one pairing.

## The rule

One rule for all three families:

> the **rms within each mHc study**, then the **max across studies**
> holding at least `MIN_STUDY_POINTS = 2` mass points in the cell,
> floored by the cell's **pooled rms**, then by an absolute floor
> (scale 0.02, res 0.01, norm 0.01).

Details:

- Points enter production cells only in the pairing production actually
  uses (highM iff mHc ≥ 100 and mA ≥ 60); the unrestricted numbers are
  kept alongside as diagnostics.
- Studies with < 2 points in a cell are skipped (a one-point study leaks
  its outlier straight back in — that would be the old max in disguise)
  and listed in `studies_below_min_points`.
- Empty-but-reachable mA bins inherit the channel's worst populated bin
  (never the bare floor) and are flagged in `warnings`. Currently:
  SR3Mu_lowM onZ — its only candidate point (MHc85_MA80) is a grid
  endpoint — inherits belowZ.
- Structurally unreachable cells (e.g. SR3Mu_lowM aboveZ: lowM requires
  mHc < 100 or mA < 60, and mA < mHc) emit no nuisance at all
  (`_reachable`).
- With the frozen model **no cell sits on any floor** — all three floors
  are pure safety nets.

## Evidence

### Why not the plain max (the previous rule)

The LOO residuals are unbiased (|mean| < 2% in every cell) and
Gaussian-like: the observed max/rms lands at 1.8–3.6 against the
√(2 ln N) = 1.8–2.9 expected for Gaussian draws. A plain max over a cell
is therefore a ~3σ order statistic used as a 1σ lnN width, scaling with
how many points the cell happens to hold rather than with model
accuracy — one mass point (MHc115_MA27) set 15 of 16 below-Z cells.
Moving to rms-then-max took the average nuisance a mass point sees from
20.7% to 15.6% (SR3Mu_lowM onZ: 29.1% → 5.3%).

### Why rms within a study is statistically valid (correlation study, 2026-08-12)

Using the rms of a cell's residuals as the 1σ error at an arbitrary new
mA assumes the residuals are **exchangeable draws** from a common error
distribution — comparable in magnitude across the cell, and each point an
approximately independent measurement. Note what it does *not* require:
correlation between mA points. Had the residuals been a smooth, highly
correlated bias curve in mA, the rms would rest on ~1 effective
measurement (unstable), and a *bias correction* rather than a symmetric
width would be the right response.

Measured on the signed LOO residuals (production pairing, pairs
restricted to one norm mA bin):

**Adjacent-in-mA correlation within a study ≈ zero or negative.**

| family | all | Run2 | Run3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| norm | −0.17 (944 pairs) | −0.28 | −0.09 |
| scale | −0.09 (236 pairs) | −0.12 | −0.04 |
| res | +0.02 (236 pairs) | +0.03 | +0.01 |

There is no smooth common bias curve in mA: each mass point is close to
an independent draw, so the per-study rms carries its full ~n effective
degrees of freedom and is a stable estimator (relative precision
~1/√(2n)). The mild *anti*-correlation is the expected LOO signature of
noise-dominated points — removing a point lets the fit relax away from
it while that point's fluctuation still pulls its neighbours' training
sets. SR3Mu_lowM norm reaches −0.44: the sparse-grid oscillation, where
consecutive LOO predictions swing with alternating signs. An envelope
built from a max would chase those swings one sign at a time; the rms
averages the oscillation into the width it actually is.

**Cross-era correlation at a fixed point is near-total** (norm): mean
r = +0.99 within Run2 (min +0.98), +0.80 within Run3 (0.64–0.96), +0.65
between Run2 and Run3. The common-mode fraction of the residual variance
across a period's four eras is 79–99% per (channel, period). So the
residual at a given (mHc, mA) is essentially ONE number per run period —
a property of the surfaces and the reference sample there — not per-era
noise. Cross-channel correlation at a fixed era is partial: +0.48 within
Run2, +0.74 within Run3 (the Run3 value is the sample-level scatter,
which is channel-correlated by construction).

Consequences:

- The per-era norm *sizes* are right — each era's rms measures the
  same common residual, which is why a period's rows are so alike in the
  table above.
- Per-era nuisance names would treat the four eras of a period as
  independent, letting them partially cancel in the period-summed signal
  yield (~×2 understatement for four equal shares) even though the
  measured error moves all four eras together. **Adopted (2026-08-12)**:
  norm is period-correlated, `CMS_interp_norm_{ch}_{13TeV|13p6TeV}`,
  matching scale/res; the per-era values survive as the per-column lnN
  entries of the one row. Run2 and Run3 stay decorrelated — their mutual
  correlation is only partial (+0.65) and the mechanisms differ (Run3
  carries the sample scatter). V1 showed interpolation nuisances cost
  ~nothing in the limit, so this is correctness, not sensitivity.

### Why max across studies (not the pooled rms alone)

Pooling all studies into one rms is validated on-Z — the observed
per-study rms spread matches the ~41% expected from finite cell sizes —
but NOT below-Z, where the observed spread is 69–77%: the per-study rms
genuinely differs with mHc, tracking low-mA grid density. MHc160 samples
every 5 GeV and closes to 2.0%; MHc115/130 have 15–25 GeV gaps and close
to 11–18%. The max across studies covers the sparse-grid studies instead
of averaging them away; the pooled rms serves as the floor.

### Why norm is mA-binned and nothing is mHc-keyed

Binning norm in mA is justified by a consistent, physically traceable
effect: below-Z is worse in every channel and era, tracking low-mA grid
density. scale shows no consistent region pattern across channels and
res sat at its (old) floor everywhere, so neither is binned. No family
keeps an mHc dependence: once mA is binned most (study, bin) cells hold
one or two points, and both models are global surfaces — a per-study max
is not an estimator there. The rms-then-max rule pools the studies
instead.

### Floors

`UNCERTAINTY_SCALE_FLOOR = 0.02`, `UNCERTAINTY_RES_FLOOR = 0.01`,
`UNCERTAINTY_NORM_FLOOR = 0.01`. The res floor was lowered 0.02 → 0.01
(2026-08-12): at 0.02 it was *setting* four of the six res cells
(measured 0.0124/0.0148/0.0179/0.0191 all pushed to 0.0200) rather than
catching degenerate ones. After the change every cell reports its
measured value; a cell landing exactly on a floor is a review trigger
([WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md) Gate C).

## Known limitations

- **Below-Z is inhomogeneous inside the bin.** Splitting belowZ at
  mA = 45 (common-mode residuals): SR1E2Mu Run2 rms 15.1% below vs 2.1%
  above; SR3Mu_lowM Run2 17.4% vs 6.3% (Run3 similar). The single
  belowZ number (~9–11% for SR1E2Mu) over-covers mA ≳ 45 and
  under-covers the lowest mA by roughly ×1.4. This is the low-mA
  grid-density limitation again — MHc115's 15 → 27 → 42 and MHc130's
  15 → 30 → 55 gaps — and one extra MC point in each gap would do more
  than a finer binning, which would only hand the sparse cells back to
  single points.
- **Run3 norm carries the upstream per-sample scatter** (8–24%), a
  property of the reference samples, deliberately absorbed rather than
  modelled.
- **SR3Mu_lowM onZ is an inherited value** (no usable point in the bin).
- **Closure pulls are uncalibrated** (assumed 1% Run2 G error vs ~5%
  surface residual); the envelopes use relative residuals and are
  unaffected.
