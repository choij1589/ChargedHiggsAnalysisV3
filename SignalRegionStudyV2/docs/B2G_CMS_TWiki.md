## Motivation

The B2G group covers models of new physics featuring heavy standard model particles in the final state (W, Z, H, boosted top or 'resonances X→top+Y’). This includes searches for diboson resonances, heavy partners of the top quark with vector-like properties (VLQ) as well as heavy resonances, such as W' and Z' resonances, and heavy neutral or charged Higgs bosons. In many of these searches the final state particles can have significant Lorentz boosts, so that their individual decay products often overlap and merge. Such boosted topologies are exploited with dedicated reconstruction algorithms that were developed and became well established in the context of the analyses of Run 1 and Run 2 data. The group maintains synergy with the related physics analyses of the HIG, TOP, [SUS](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/SUS), and EXO PAGs.

#### Contacts mandates

-   [PPD](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/PPD)/NanoAOD: the contact will make sure that analyses are using the latest recommendations from [PPD](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/PPD) following up with issues on data taking and reporting them to the b2g meetings and hypernews.

-   Generators: the contact will take care of helping analysts with the signal or background production, present and discuss our requests to the MC coordination meetings, as well as participate the review for what concerns simulation samples usage.

-   Triggers: the position involves following recent development and requests by the TSG, monitoring the trigger efficiencies and the trigger rates during data taking. The contact will review the trigger usage in analyses and will help analysts to develop trigger strategies from early on in the analysis stage, ensure they are using the optimal trigger for their use case, and give them guidance so that they can monitor the triggers they are using and their performance.

-   EGM+MUO: the contact will follow up on the muon usage in the analyses, and report any development from the Muon POG, as well as potential issues. The contact will also check any electron/photon related issue with the analyses, helping review the correct usage of electrons/photons and respective scale factors.

-   TAU: the contact will follow up on the tau usage in the analyses, and report any development from the Tau POG, as well as potential issues.

-   JME: the contact will follow up on the jet and MET usage in the analyses, and including vector boson, top, and higgs jet usage.

-   BTV: the contacts will follow up on the b-tagging usage in the analyses, this includes standard b-jet tagging, sub-jet tagging, double-taggers and other techniques.

-   Data Quality Monitoring and Validation: the contact will monitor development in [PPD](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/PPD) towards Run3 and keep up-to-date monitoring tools, such that most important quantities relevant to [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) (in particular boosted objects) are under control for Run3. The contact person will perform release validation and similar [DQM](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/DQM) activities. EPR may be provided for release validation work.

-   HEPData: the contact should work on managing the HEPData submissions together with the contact authors of analyses. This involves creating HEPData entries, discussing the content to be uploaded to HEPData and helping with the conversion into the HEPData format. Ensuring that our analyses become available on HEPData after publication is a crucial endeavor for a search group, making this position a high profile task. The candidate will crucially affect the impact of our publications and have high visibility, also beyond our group.

-   Statistics/combine: the contact will take care of helping analysts with statistics related questions, as well as participate the review for what concerns statistics and combine datacards.

-   Machine Learning: the contact will take care of helping analysts with machine learning related questions and maintain dialogue with the machine learning group. The contact will also check machine learning usage in the analyses reviews.

## New analysis contributions and other free positions

The [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) group almost always has places where new contributions are welcome. Please contact the conveners if you have ideas, and we will definitely be able to find you something interesting to work on, that will most likely result in a CMS paper. The B2G group works in an open structure where any group is allowed to **work together** with other teams. Competing analyses, while sometimes necessary, are to first order discouraged as there are quite a few open topics that also need our attention. If multiple analyses on the same topic and final state exist, we generally encourage collaboration, even between different techniques.

We also value contributions within the structure of the [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) PAG, and have plenty of leadership opportunities.

### Luminosity and pileup reweighting procedures

All the relevant information to implement luminosity uncertainties are given in the [LumiRecommendationsRun2 TWiki](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/LumiRecommendationsRun2). Please keep in mind that the luminosity uncertainty values provided in the summary table there are not meant to be used in actual analyses. Analyzers are supposed to implement the correct correlation scheme described under the "Combination and correlations" section. This means that 5 nuisance parameters of lnN type should be implemented: 3 NPs for uncorrelated uncertainties, 1 NP for the correlated uncertainty across all three years, and 1 NP for the correlated uncertainty between 2017 and 2018. Thus, your datacards should look something like

```
lumi_13TeV_Uncorrelated_2016_preVFP                 lnN   1.01 1.01 1.01 1.01 1.01 -  1.01 1.01 1.01 1.01 1.01 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated                               lnN   1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 - 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated_1718                          lnN   - - - - - - - - - - - - 
```

for 2016 (pre or post VFP),

```
lumi_13TeV_Uncorrelated_2017                        lnN   1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 - 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated                               lnN   1.009 1.009 1.009 1.009 1.009 - 1.009 1.009 1.009 1.009 1.009 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated_1718                          lnN   1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 - 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 1.006 -
```

for 2017, and

```
lumi_13TeV_Uncorrelated_2018                        lnN   1.015 1.015 1.015 1.015 1.015 - 1.015 1.015 1.015 1.015 1.015 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated                               lnN   1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 - 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 1.020 -
lumi_13TeV_Correlated_1718                          lnN   1.002 1.002 1.002 1.002 1.002 - 1.002 1.002 1.002 1.002 1.002 -
```

for 2018 respectively. You are allowed to use the values reported in the summary table if and only if you are merging templates from all three years into a single datacard. Anyway, please note that this is not the standard procedure and it should be agreed with L3 and L2 conveners.

An implementation of pileup reweighting and of the related uncertainties is available in correctionlib. A pythonic template code (thanks to Garvita Agarwal) may look something like

```
def GetPUSF(IOV, nTrueInt, var='nominal'):
    ## json files from: https://gitlab.cern.ch/cms-nanoAOD/jsonpog-integration/-/tree/master/POG/LUM
    fname = "data/pileup/puWeights_UL"+IOV+".json.gz"
    hname = {
        "2016APV": "Collisions16_UltraLegacy_goldenJSON",
        "2016"   : "Collisions16_UltraLegacy_goldenJSON",
        "2017"   : "Collisions17_UltraLegacy_goldenJSON",
        "2018"   : "Collisions18_UltraLegacy_goldenJSON"
    }
    evaluator = correctionlib.CorrectionSet.from_file(fname)
    return evaluator[hname[IOV]].evaluate(np.array(nTrueInt), var)


```

If you are running on [NanoAOD](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/NanoAOD), please make sure you are using the Pileup\_nTrueInt variable to perform the reweighting (as it is correctly done in the snippet above).

### Analysis Reviews and Pre-approval Procedure

-   The [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) PAG requires an explicit green-light for each and every analysis from the "Object Experts". To facilitate this, the conveners will create a page for the internal PAG review of the analysis based on the template here:
    -   [B2GXXYYYReview template](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2GXXYYYReview)

-   Instructions for Analysts:
    1.  Provide the AN and Paper in as complete a form as possible at least **two weeks** before your scheduled preapproval. The object reviews and convener decisions about green-lights will happen in that week.
    2.  green-light is given for preapproval only after object contacts and conveners are satisfied with the answers.
    3.  If green-light is given for preapproval: Documentation is frozen **one week** before preapproval date with relevant textual updates to the AN and Paper.
    4.  Any changes to the documentation from the group and object expert review must be incorporated **before the ARC review begins** so they can accurately assess the readiness. Paper quality and readiness for review are amongst the conditions for pre-approval.
    5.  Propose a target journal on the analysis hypernews copying [cms-publication-committee-chair@cern.ch](mailto:cms-publication-committee-chair@cern.ch) - at the same time, please also propose a CCLE (see [https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/viewauth/CMS/Internal/CcleList](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/viewauth/CMS/Internal/CcleList))
-   Instructions for Object Experts:
    1.  Read the sections of the AN of the analysis in question (minimally those sections that pertain to your area of expertise).
    2.  Give feedback on anything that is not clear.
    3.  Facilitate communication with the POG if needed.
    4.  Once questions are answered by the analysts to your satisfaction, update the table in the appropriate TWiki with your name, and change the status to Green-light given.
        -   **The green-light is expected BEFORE preapproval.**
    5.  The green-light for preapproval is contingent upon the green-lights of the object experts, or convener override in exceptional cases (for instance, due to illness, vacations, etc).
-   The preapproval procedure is detailed at [https://indico.cern.ch/event/883175/contributions/3721492/attachments/1993206/3324233/New\_B2G\_unblinding\_policy.pdf![](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/pub/TWiki/TWikiDocGraphics/external-link.gif)](https://indico.cern.ch/event/883175/contributions/3721492/attachments/1993206/3324233/New_B2G_unblinding_policy.pdf)
    1.  The analysis remains blinded
    2.  After the pre-approval presentation, the possible homework needs to be addressed
    3.  Unblinding can happen (at the explicit request of the [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) conveners) at the earliest one week after the preapproval presentation, or when the pre-approval homework is done, whichever is later. The procedure is as follows:
        1.  Perform F test also including the signal region (only if your analysis involves a choice of fit function for the background estimation)
        2.  Obtain unblinded impacts and Goodness-of-fit tests - note that if you are using the standard Impacts workflow in combine, you should add the option --blind when using the plotImpacts.py script to avoid showing the signal strength at this step.
        3.  Obtain unblinded signal region distributions (with pre-fit+post-fit background only)
        4.  Derive observed limits (and post-fit expected limits) and signal strength/significance for the largest deviation (excess/deficit) observed
    4.  Pre-approval will be given following the unblinding and after possible further investigations needed
-   The ARC will take over after the group gives the preapproval, and will guide the analysts through the next phase.
    1.  The documentation (AN and paper/PAS) must be frozen **one week** before the approval deadline.
    2.  The ARC chair gives the green-light for approval.
    3.  The analysis is then presented at a [B2G](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/B2G) meeting, and the approval decision is made.
-   Instructions for supplementary material :
    1.  See [here![](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/pub/TWiki/TWikiDocGraphics/external-link.gif)](https://ghm.web.cern.ch/ghm/plots/AdditionalMaterial.html) for details.
    2.  Relevant summary : Add "CMS Supplementary" to all plots (check latest label recommendation [here](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/edit/Internal/FigGuidelines?topicparent=CMS.B2G;nowysiwyg=1 "this topic does not yet exist; you can create it.")), and ARC and the PC / conveners must approve the materials.

## Special topics

There are a number of topics that are very useful for the B2G PAG. Technical contributions are handled through the respective POGs. They include:

-   Jet substructure (JME)
-   B-tagging in merged jets (BTV)
-   Non-isolated leptons (EGM, MUO, TAU)

-   Scale factors for efficiency and JES :
    -   The recommended working points and scale factors for W-tagging are here: [W taggers](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/JetWtagging) [Top taggers](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/CMS/JetTopTagging)
    -   For higgs boson tagging, in general one uses similar scale factors as for the W-taggers.
    -   The mis-tagging of backgrounds can be an important uncertainty, please consider either evaluating it or the effect of mis-tagged backgrounds via data-driven methods!
    -   **Other taggers and algorithms with differing cuts must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis!** Examples are multivariate classifiers that provide scores for different channels.
